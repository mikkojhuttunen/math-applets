Fiber parametric amplifier

A 532 nm pump amplifies a signal near 1550 nm in a second-order nonlinear fiber and creates an idler near 810 nm. The coupled amplitude equations are integrated with RK4, including pump depletion, phase mismatch, Gaussian mode overlap and optional dissipation of the idler.

Model

Amplitudes aj(z) are normalised so that |aj|² is the photon flux (photons/s) in mode j. With a continuous-wave, slowly varying envelope and a single transverse mode per wave:

dap/dz = iK as ai e−iΔkz
das/dz = iK ap ai* e+iΔkz
dai/dz = iK ap as* e+iΔkz − (αi/2) ai
K = 2 deff √( ħ ωp ωs ωi / (2 ε0 c³ np ns ni) ) · Θ
Θ = ∫fpfsfi dA / √(∫fp² ∫fs² ∫fi²) = π / (Σ wj−2) / √((π/2)³ wp² ws² wi²)

Here fj = exp(−r²/wj²) and Aeff = 1/Θ². The small-signal gain coefficient is Γ = K√Φp(0) with Φp = Pp/ħωp. Without loss and with an undepleted pump, G = 1 + (Γ/g)² sinh²(gL), g² = Γ² − (Δk/2)².

The dashed curves solve the linearised signal–idler pair exactly (2×2 matrix exponential, with lumped dumps applied as jumps). With strong continuous idler loss (αi ≫ Γ) the idler follows the signal adiabatically and the signal sees a Lorentzian gain coefficient gs = Γ²αi / ((αi/2)² + Δk²). The gain becomes smaller but much less sensitive to Δk.

  • Refractive index n = 1.45 for all three waves (fused silica); it only enters K. Dispersion is lumped into Δk.
  • Pump and signal are lossless. Without idler seed the gain does not depend on the input phases.
  • The sum Φp + Φs is conserved even with idler loss; its drift is reported as the numerical error.
  • Step size adapts to max(Γ√(1 + Φs/Φp), |Δk|, αi/2). The gain scan uses at most 20 000 steps per point.
